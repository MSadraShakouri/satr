## math

- [x] math syntax checking while typing would be great, also colors and highlighting, learn from obsidian (maybe change the math things to be closer to Obsidian) — *both halves, and past Obsidian on the colouring. A fence is colourful because a language has many **kinds** of word; `stex` — the mode Obsidian runs over its own math source — has almost one, so matching Obsidian exactly left a formula at two hues and some grey, sitting under a fence that uses seven. LaTeX does have the kinds; they are in **which command it is**, not in the token name. So a backslash word is sorted: scaffolding (`\begin \end \left \right \displaystyle`) `--code-keyword`, a command that takes something (`\frac \sqrt \sum \int \text`) `--code-function`, a command that IS something (`\alpha \infty \to \leq`) `--code-important`; the words inside `\text{…}` and the environment named by `\begin{…}` `--code-string`; and the rest on Obsidian's own map, read out of its app.css — numbers `--code-value`, variables `--code-property`, `_ ^ & \\` `--code-tag`, `+ - = <` `--code-operator`, braces `--code-punctuation`, `%` to end of line `--code-comment`. A letter is **any** letter, not A-Z: a Persian formula, or a Greek character typed straight in rather than spelled `\alpha`, was left with no colour at all by a Latin-only test. Arabic-script runs read as words rather than variables, because renderMath wraps them in `\text{…}` before KaTeX sees them. And the half Obsidian does not do at all: a formula KaTeX cannot read says so in colour, never with a squiggle — a faint red behind the whole formula, its dollars in that red, and the one piece KaTeX choked on in a deeper red. A complaint with no width (a bare `\frac` is reported at the end of the formula with a length of zero) is pinned on the last token before the point, so `\frac` itself is marked rather than the formula going red with nothing in it named. **Nothing waits for anything.** There were two goes at "do not call a formula wrong while it is being written" — a 0.7s pause after the last keystroke, then a test for the backslash word the caret was in the middle of — and both failed in the same place, which is that the caret is inside the bad word exactly when the writer has gone there to look at it: measured, in `$\fracc{1}{2}$` the mark was gone for every caret position from the `f` to the `{`. So every formula is judged every time it changes and what KaTeX says is shown, caret or no caret; a command is undefined for the four keystrokes it takes to spell `\alpha`, and that is the honest cost of a verdict that is never wrong about when it speaks. A test now sweeps every caret offset in a broken formula and fails if any of them hides the mark. The reason itself is said at the **top of the screen**, for as long as the caret is in the broken formula — not on the bar at the bottom, because the bar is where the hands are and a line written at the bottom of the screen ends up behind the very complaint it caused. One line, so KaTeX's words are translated into the fewest that still name the thing to fix: *"Unexpected end of input in a macro argument, expected '}' at end of input: \frac"* is **`\frac is missing its {…}`**, *"Undefined control sequence: \alfa"* is **`\alfa is not a command`**, and so on for every shape KaTeX actually produces — a stray `}`, a `{` never closed, a `\begin` with no `\end`, two `^` on one thing. Anything unlisted passes through in KaTeX's own words rather than being guessed at.*
- [x] math split lines needs a bit thought, it's great overall but in some places it's not great — *Right, and the places it was not great turned out to be measurable rather than a matter of taste. Two corpora, no invented examples: 4 477 formulas scraped out of Wikipedia's maths and physics articles, and 819 out of your own uni-notes repo, every one rendered into a 340 px column — a phone held upright. Before: Wikipedia painted 294 formulas past the edge and handed 168 of them back to the browser whole; your notes had 36 off the screen, 5 408 px of overrun between them, the worst single line 552 px past the edge. The first defect was that `+ − · ×` were not break points at all, so `a + b + c + … = 0` was cut into “everything” and “= 0” and “everything” went off the side. A sum may now break at its own operators, last of all — after line ends, commas, relations and word spaces — and the operator ends the line rather than starting the next one, because a line opening with `+` reads as a sign. A sign is told from an operator by what sits either side of it, so `x = -y`, `(+1) + (-1)`, `\pm 5` and `R^+ \times R^-` are left whole. That fixed Wikipedia and did nothing whatever for your notes — 10 bad formulas before, 10 after — because your hard ones are a different shape: tuples and vectors inside `\left( … \right)`, which had no break point anywhere inside them. So there is now a last resort: a line may be cut inside brackets, closing with `\right.` and opening the next with `\left.`, so each piece is still a formula in itself and the brackets still look the way you typed them. `\quad` and `\qquad` are break points too — your Persian definition lines (`Z : \text{تابع هدف} \quad x_j : \text{…}`) had nothing else to break at. And a formula whose `\\` belongs to an embedded `\begin{vmatrix}` is no longer frozen whole: `\det A = \begin{vmatrix}…\end{vmatrix} = 12` breaks at its `=` signs now, with the matrix itself kept intact on its own line. After: your notes 36 → 9 off the screen, 5 408 px → 627 px; Wikipedia 168 → 50 handed back and 51 → 19 off the screen, counting everything that is not an aligned environment. Those are set aside on purpose, as you asked — a `\begin{align}` or a matrix is your own layout and still scrolls sideways rather than being taken apart. What is left over is five formulas too wide inside a single `\frac` or `\sqrt`, which cannot be broken without rewriting the maths, and three aligned blocks. The cost is about 0.23 ms per formula. 44 awkward formulas written by hand — decimal commas against list commas, `1,000,000`, `x,,y`, `a، b`, Greek typed straight in, degenerate things like a lone `+` or `a +` — are a permanent spec now, so none of this can quietly come undone.*
- [x] maybe remove the spaces thing of math, I usually use \text{} and I believe that handles it, needs feedback — *Removed, and you were right. Spaces inside a formula are TeX's spaces again, which is to say they are ignored: `$a b$` and `$ab$` render identically, as they do everywhere else LaTeX is read. Words come from `\text{…}`, and the spaces inside a `\text{}` are kept — they are also the second-best place to break a long line, so `\text{if } x > 0 \text{ then } y = 1` still wraps between its words. A run of Persian is still wrapped in `\text{}` for you before KaTeX sees it, so its spaces survive by the same rule. The only loser is an old note that leaned on bare spaces to hold words apart inside maths; it will close up.*

## launch and scrolling

- [x] app launch is slow on big files even after "away time", I think maybe the order of checking is bad
- [x] scroll up sometimes is weird, it still bounces, going down a tiny bit fixes it but it bounces when going up (this thing changed so many times but never fully got resolved) — *found, and it was never the page: it is the bars. Reproduced by measurement — a flick up shows them, then a scroll correction of about 130px takes them away again. The editor makes that correction when it re-measures line heights it had only estimated above the viewport, and it moves `scrollTop` precisely so that the text on screen does NOT move, which is why nothing visible ever jumped. Read off `scrollTop` alone it is indistinguishable from turning around. The bars are told from the reader's hand now (a finger on the glass, a wheel, a scrolling key); momentum and corrections cannot turn them around, and a real swipe down still hides them at once. Left unticked until it has had a week on the phone. **Still reported after that**, and now with the shape of it: slowly, upwards, just after the app opens, and after switching tabs — on the website too. Two answers: a slow thumb is never straight, so turning the bars around takes 10px of finger travel the other way now, not the last two pixels; and `?bars=1` on the website puts the last ten decisions on the screen (what was asked, how far the note moved, which way the hand was going, what was refused), because the phone cannot be inspected from here. If the trace shows nothing while the bar still moves, the bar is not ours to move — Chrome's URL bar and Android's transient status bar both come and go on their own, and that is the next thing to rule out. **Then: fixed again, the third way.** The beat that keeps the two bar moves apart was 200ms and the bars animate `transform` over 300ms, so a reversal arriving in between found the beat over and restarted the slide half way through — a flash no gesture rule could ever catch, because the question there is not which way the reader went but whether the bar has finished moving. The beat is 340 now (the transform plus a frame), and the gate a reversal has to clear is one body line, 24px, instead of 12 — a thumb resting on the glass wobbles through 12. Raising it exposed a double charge: `onNavScroll` already spends NAV_HELD before asking, and `flushNavigation` then measured again from where the note had got to, so a 40px scroll up had 16px left and was refused; the held move is measured from the anchor now, which is where the bars last moved. Both follow-ups are now done. The 340 is gone: the bars say when they have stopped moving, through a `transitionend` listener on the `transform` of `.topbar` and `.mobile-navbar`, so the stylesheet and the scroll rules cannot drift apart again — and the timer that remains is only a backstop for a bar that is `display:none` or a phone set to reduce motion, measured from the elements' own computed duration rather than copied by hand. The gate is asymmetric: taking the bars away is the move you cannot undo without another gesture, so it takes **32px**, while bringing them back costs nothing and takes **16px** — still three times the 4-6px a thumb wobbles while it rests, and under the deliberate 20px nudge that the single 24px gate used to swallow. The Java guard cannot help, and says so now in a comment — `isVisible()` reads false for the whole of a hide animation, so a show() arriving mid-slide is not filtered there and never could be. A test shortens the slide to 60ms and fails if the held move does not land sooner for it.*

## note lint criteria

for the in-app lint/fixer above, measured on term_three with the app's own scanner (0 katex errors today, everything else is real):

1. a display `$$` line that is a lazy continuation of a list item — it renders inside the bullet, indented and centered within the `li`, instead of as a page-centered display block. 11 sites now: Probability Theory 03:83,86,89,92,95,121,124,127,130; Mathematics 2 03:32; Linear Algebra 01:18. fix = one blank line before the `$$`
2. `<div dir=ltr>` wrappers are now redundant (direction is computed, math is already forced ltr) — 20 pairs in Mathematics 2 01, all paired, safe to strip. report as "redundant", not "wrong"
3. trailing `:` in a heading, visible in the outline and in search — Accounting 03:5 `## فصل دوم: `, Accounting 03:66 `##### اهداف:`
4. trailing space in heading text, so the outline reads `معرفی ` — Accounting 01:14, Linear Algebra 02:21
5. list indent must be 0/2/4: a 1-space indent reads as a sibling rather than a child, so a bullet that looks like a parent renders as a peer — Probability Theory 02:246
6. empty list item renders as a blank bullet — Probability Theory 01:159
7. run every formula through `renderMath` and fail on any `katex-error` — katex is `throwOnError: false`, so a typo is a silent red formula in the reading view and in the PDF, and there is no other way to notice
8. assert the latex-style converter never fires on a real line break: `\\[6pt]` in Linear Algebra 03:38 is a `\\[6pt]` skip, not a `\[…\]` pair. today correct by two independent guards (the preceding backslash, and the enclosing `$$` span) — worth pinning so neither guard is ever weakened

not criteria, deliberately: callouts/dataview/graph/tags (not supported, would render literally), table separator styles and headerless tables (`ensureTableSeparators` inserts the separator, then the empty header row is removed), persian `۱.` ordered lists (transliterated, then re-rendered as persian), bare emails (gfm autolinks them), math digit style (a settings choice), and unifying `$$ x $$` against bare `$$` (both are `display: true`, they render the same)
